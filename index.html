Abstract

Reducing the intelligibility of background speech, one of the most disruptive sources of noise pollution in open-plan offices, is a challenge. Active noise cancellation techniques are challenged by speech's rapid spectro-temporal variations, while static sound-masking systems must operate at high levels to be effective, increasing overall noise exposure. We propose GranSpeechMask, a low-latency granular speech-masking method that reduces the intelligibility of distracting speech by broadcasting short segments of the target speaker's own recent speech. Unlike ANC, GranSpeechMask does not require extremely low latency, and unlike static sound-masking systems, it dynamically optimizes the level of the masking signal. Evaluated on monospeaker and multispeaker simulated office speech datasets, GranSpeechMask substantially outperforms a speech-shaped-noise baseline and demonstrates good robustness to multispeaker scenes.

2. Effect of the VAD detection threshold

GranSpeechMask relies on a voice activity detector (VAD) to determine when to trigger concealing. Below a threshold of about 0.4, TEN VAD starts accepting non-speech audio as speech, causing the system to trigger concealing on non-speech segments and potentially select non-speech clips as concealers. A threshold of 0.5, used throughout the paper, lies on the Pareto front between intelligibility and emergence. Higher thresholds also provide acceptable performance. Compared with 0.5, a threshold of 0.9 reduces concealer emergence but also increases speech intelligibility.

3. Comparison to SSN baseline

At the Mid EBR–SAR condition, GranSpeechMask is compared with the speech-shaped-noise (SSN) baseline. SSN introduces a constant, speech-shaped noise throughout the clip, whereas GranSpeechMask conceals speech selectively by detecting speech activity and replaying short segments of the speaker's own recent voice.

6. Multispeaker robustness

GranSpeechMask is evaluated on SOS-2SP, a two-speaker pseudo-conversation dataset, at the Mid EBR–SAR condition. The SPI Oracle variant forces the concealer to be selected from the correct speaker's past speech using ground-truth speaker identity, whereas GranSpeechMask selects concealers solely based on log-Mel spectral similarity, without knowing which speaker is currently speaking. The performance gap between the two approaches is small overall, but informal listening suggests that the SPI Oracle seem to perform better in terms of naturalness.
